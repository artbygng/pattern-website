A crochet PDF pattern is just an ordinary document, so you can print it on any home printer with no special setup. You can also skip printing entirely and read it on your phone or tablet, which is what a lot of makers do. Unlike a PDF sewing pattern, there are no tiled pages to tape together and no scaling to get exactly right. It's pages of written instructions and photos, like printing anything else.

Here are some questions that come up from time to time:

## Where do I find my pattern after I buy it?

Your pattern is in your Etsy account under Purchases, with a View Downloads button next to the order. As soon as your payment clears (usually a minute or two), the file is ready and you can download it. Etsy will also send you an email with a link to the files.

## Why can't I download my pattern on Etsy?

One thing comes up a lot: you can't download a digital file through the Etsy phone app. That's the Etsy app functionality. If the download button isn't there, you're almost certainly in the app — open Etsy in your phone's web browser (Safari or Chrome) or on a computer instead, and the button should be there. You can re-download as many times as you like, so a lost file is never really lost.

## Do I have to print the pattern?

No — you can if you want to, but plenty of makers read them on their tablets, computers, or phones. Some adjustments might be needed when reading on your phone, like zooming into the section you're working on, but generally patterns can read comfortably on a screen, so a tablet or phone propped next to your yarn works perfectly and saves you the paper and ink.

Printing (or not) is a preference. Some people like a paper copy they can scribble notes on and check off rows; others like keeping their place on a screen. Neither is more "correct" — do whichever suits how you work.

## How do I print a crochet PDF pattern at home?

Open the file in a PDF reader, choose Print, and print at full size — that's the whole job. On a computer, download the file and open it in Adobe Acrobat Reader, Preview (Mac), or your browser's built-in PDF viewer, then print as you would any document. Patterns will usually be written on A4 pages, so printing should be simple. If you see a "Fit to page" or "Fit to printable area" option, leaving it on is a safe choice that keeps anything from getting cut off at the margins.

Two easy ways to save ink, if you want them:

- **Print only the pages you need.** You don't have to print all of it. You can skip the cover and intro pages if you can remember the [abbreviations, special stitches and such](/blog/how-to-read-a-crochet-pattern), and print just the instruction pages.
- **Print in grayscale.** The photos are usually there to support the stitching and construction instructions, so black-and-white printing should read well and stretches your color cartridge. Choose "Black and white" or "Grayscale" in your printer settings.

## Reading the pattern on a tablet or phone

To use the pattern on a device while you crochet, download and save it to your phone, tablet, or computer. Save it somewhere you'll remember, and go back and read it whenever you like. You can turn your screen's auto-lock up to a few minutes so it doesn't keep going dark mid-round, and you've got a hands-free pattern that never blows shut like a paper page does (just don't forget to turn it back to its original setting when you're done).

Whichever way you like to work — paper or screen — your pattern is ready to download the moment you buy it.
